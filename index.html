    What the browser can and cannot do

    In R (the course scripts)
    • quadprog::solve.QP() solves minimum variance exactly.
    • PortfolioAnalytics::optimize.portfolio() wraps maximum Sharpe.
    • TTR::runCor(), runSD(), zoo::rollapply() drive the rolling views.
    Here in the browser
    • Inverse volatility needs no solver — the handout's stand-in. It ignores correlation.
    • Minimum variance and maximum Sharpe run through quadprog — a JavaScript port of the very same Goldfarb–Idnani dual method that solve.QP() uses in R. Same algorithm, same answer, no server.
    • Maximum Sharpe reaches the solver through the standard reformulation: minimise y'Σy subject to (μ−rf)'y = 1, then rescale w = y/Σy. That is the "quadprog reformulation" the handout offers as optional depth.
    • Projected gradient descent stays as a fallback for the cases a QP genuinely cannot answer — a position cap too tight to be feasible, or no asset out-earning the risk-free rate.
    • A weighting is never shipped from an optimiser straight to a trading system. That missing human step is what ended Knight Capital in 45 minutes.
